\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATIONS}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}\label{sec:setup}

The scenario, constraint, and mission parameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:scenario}. The target flies on a $500$-km circular orbit with an inclination of $45^{\circ}$, and both spacecraft are propagated with \eqref{eq:kin}--\eqref{eq:fg} by an adaptive Runge--Kutta integrator; no other disturbance is applied. The target is a homogeneous box with a grapple rod leading to the port, tumbling freely at a spin rate between $2.1$ and $2.7^{\circ}$/s about an axis that deviates by $10$--$20^{\circ}$ from its body $z$-axis, so that $\Ts\in[133,171]$~s. Its initial attitude is uniformly distributed on $\SO$. The chaser starts in a uniformly random direction at a range of $12$--$16$~m, with a relative speed of at most $0.05$~m/s, a boresight within $15^{\circ}$ of the port, and per-axis angular rates of at most $0.005$~rad/s.

Two mission types are considered. Urgent missions have short deadlines and price time; economy missions have longer deadlines and a tighter budget and price propellant. The economy budget was selected by a prescreening with non-learning stage policies: at this budget, the propellant constraint binds in a substantial share of tasks, while every prescreened task is still captured by at least one of these policies. Fig.~\ref{fig:timescales} compares the latest admissible entry times with the spin period: for urgent missions, $\te$ is comparable to one spin period, and for economy missions to two. Training draws urgent missions with probability $3/4$.

The execution-layer and learning parameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:params}. They are the same for all learning-based methods compared below.
