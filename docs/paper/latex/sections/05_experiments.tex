\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATIONS}\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates ECC and RWG on capture of the tumbling target under a deadline. The simulations address three questions: whether ECC captures the target more often than a learning-guided MPC trained in the same hierarchy (Section~\ref{sec:main}); how much each of the two channels of ECC contributes and how screening acts at a decision (Section~\ref{sec:attribution}); and what RWG contributes and how the learned decision layer compares with non-learning stage policies (Section~\ref{sec:rwg-results}). A representative capture (Section~\ref{sec:case}) shows the phases that RWG and the execution layer produce before their components are evaluated, and Section~\ref{sec:learning-curves} reports the learning process.

\subsection{Simulation Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textit{1) Scenario:} The scenario, constraint, and task parameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:scenario}. The target flies on a $500$-km circular orbit with an inclination of $45^{\circ}$, and both spacecraft are propagated with \eqref{eq:kin}--\eqref{eq:fg} by an adaptive Runge--Kutta integrator. The target is a homogeneous box with a grapple rod leading to the port and tumbles freely at a spin rate between $2.1$ and $2.7^{\circ}$/s about an axis that deviates by $10$--$20^{\circ}$ from its body $z$-axis, so that $\Ts\in[133, 171]$~s. Its initial attitude is uniformly distributed on $\SO$. The chaser starts in a uniformly random direction at a range of $12$--$16$~m, with a relative speed of at most $0.05$~m/s, a boresight within $15^{\circ}$ of the port, and per-axis angular rates of at most $0.005$~rad/s. With deadlines of $140$--$200$~s, the latest admissible entry time $\te$ lies between $95$ and $155$~s and is thus comparable to one spin period: a missed pass costs about as much time as the entire interval available for the entry.

\textit{2) Learning methods:} The four learning methods of Table~\ref{tab:methods} form a $2\times2$ design over the two channels of ECC. SAC--MPC trains the hierarchy of Section~\ref{sec:framework} with SAC, a standard critic $Q(\bo,\ba)$, and no screening. ECC w/o screening adds the execution feedback to the critics only, and ECC w/o feedback adds screening only. All four share the execution layer, the observation, the action space, the training distribution, and the number of decisions; the critics that read $\bz$ differ only in their input layer. Each method is trained from scratch with three random seeds for $5000$ decisions under the parameters of Table~\ref{tab:params}. A training task follows the setting of Table~\ref{tab:scenario} with probability $3/4$; otherwise its deadline is drawn from $\mathcal{U}(280,340)$~s, its budget is $2.0$~m/s, and its weights are $c_{\mathrm{t}}=0.02$~s$^{-1}$ and $c_{\mathrm{f}}=40$~s/m, with the indicator $\iota$ in \eqref{eq:obs} identifying the two weightings. \todo{TD3--MPC: the same hierarchy trained with TD3 is added to Table~\ref{tab:methods} and Table~\ref{tab:main} when its results are available.}

\textit{3) Non-learning baselines:} Five stage policies without learning delimit the roles of the layers. Pure MPC applies the near-field MPC of Section~\ref{sec:executor} from the initial instant, with its setpoint at the capture point rotating with the target and without a stage layer. The fixed intent $\ba=[1,0]^{\T}$ closes at full pace and waits for the natural sweep of the port. The deadline rule closes at a pace proportional to the range and turns toward the oncoming port at full along-sweep speed once waiting for the natural pass would leave too little time before the deadline. Direct entry planning invokes the entry planner of Section~\ref{sec:entry} from the far field and plans again whenever an entry is lost, so it searches entry trajectories directly rather than through the two-dimensional intent. The rotation-unaware approach removes the latitude regulation and the along-sweep component from the stage goal and closes along the line of sight with $a_{\mathrm{p}}=1$. Except for pure MPC, all baselines act through RWG and the execution layer.

\textit{4) Evaluation:} Each trained policy is evaluated with its mean action on $96$ test initial conditions that are not used in training, giving $288$ test tasks per method over the three seeds. Because all methods are evaluated on the same initial conditions, every comparison between them is paired; $95\%$ confidence intervals of paired differences are obtained by a bootstrap that resamples the initial conditions within each seed ($10\,000$ resamples). The non-learning baselines are evaluated on $48$ initial conditions drawn from the same distribution. A task ends in capture, timeout, propellant exhaustion, or an unsafe state (Section~\ref{sec:mdp}). The capture rate and the return $G$ of the unshaped reward \eqref{eq:reward} measure performance; the completion time and the velocity increment are compared on the tasks captured by both methods of a pair, and capture-rate differences are given in percentage points (pp).

The four learning methods are compared on the $288$ test tasks (Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:main}). ECC captures $236$ tasks ($81.9\%$) and attains the highest mean return $G$, followed by ECC w/o feedback, ECC w/o screening, and SAC--MPC with $204$ captures ($70.8\%$). Nearly all failures are timeouts: no method exhausts its propellant, and the two partial variants each end once in an unsafe state. The captured tasks are completed in similar times and with similar propellant, with medians of $131.0$--$133.4$~s and $1.23$--$1.32$~m/s across the four methods. Under the deadline, the methods thus differ in how often they convert a pass of the port into a capture in time.

Paired on the same tasks, ECC raises the capture rate over SAC--MPC by $11.1$~pp ($95\%$ CI $[6.6, 16.0]$) and the return by $21.6$ $[12.2, 31.4]$. Fig.~\ref{fig:main}(b) shows where the difference arises: $43$ tasks are captured by ECC alone and $11$ by SAC--MPC alone. On the $193$ tasks captured by both, the completion time differs by $-0.5$~s $[-1.9, 1.0]$ and the velocity increment by $+0.04$~m/s $[0.02, 0.07]$, about $3\%$ of the median. ECC therefore gains by turning timeouts into captures, with the completion time of a capture unchanged and slightly more propellant. The gain is $+12$ and $+21$ captures in two seeds and $-1$ in the third, in which SAC--MPC attains its highest capture count.

\textit{1) Ablation over the channels:} Each edge of the $2\times2$ design in Fig.~\ref{fig:attribution}(a) adds one channel to a method trained from scratch, so the paired difference along an edge measures that channel in the presence or absence of the other. Screening raises the capture rate in both settings: by $7.3$~pp $[2.8, 11.8]$ without the execution feedback and by $8.7$~pp $[4.5, 12.8]$ with it, the latter positive in all three seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:attribution}(b)). In the comparison of ECC with ECC w/o screening, $33$ tasks are captured only with screening and $8$ only without it. By construction, screening acts only at decisions where one intent separates capture from timeout, and there it replaces a proposal whose predicted course reaches no entry or descent by an admissible candidate.

The execution feedback raises the capture rate by $3.8$~pp $[0.3, 7.3]$ when screening is present, whereas without screening its effect of $2.4$~pp $[-1.7, 6.6]$ is not distinguishable from zero. The two channels are therefore complementary, consistent with the role of the feedback: it informs the critics whether the execution layer sees an opportunity and how the previous intent was executed, which pays off in the closed loop in which screening converts the opportunities into entries. Together they yield the $11.1$-pp gain of ECC over SAC--MPC, with the four methods ordered $204$, $211$, $225$, and $236$ captures.

\textit{2) Screening at a decision:} Fig.~\ref{fig:screen-case} shows one test task, with a deadline of $159.9$~s, on which screening decides the outcome. Screening corrected three of the six decisions of ECC. At $48$~s, the policy proposed $\ba=[0.99,-0.61]^{\T}$; the mode prediction of this intent contained neither an entry nor a descent within $\Tp$. Six of the nine candidates were admissible, each with an entry predicted within $4$--$6$~s, and the correction map selected the nearest one, $\atil=[1,0]^{\T}$, with an entry predicted at $54$~s (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen-case}(a)). The entry transfer started at $54$~s as predicted. The two earlier corrections, at $0$ and $32$~s, likewise replaced proposals without a predicted event by intents with a predicted entry and kept the chaser closing at full pace; at $48$~s, its range was $10.8$~m. The entry transfer was re-timed once and lost at $82$~s; after a further stage, the descent started at $114$~s, $4$~s before the pass of the port, and the target was captured at $156.4$~s.

On the same task, SAC--MPC proposed intents with closing components between $-0.25$ and $0.63$ during the first $48$~s and reached that instant at a range of $14.0$~m. It met no entry condition, started the descent from stage flight at $116$~s, and reached the deadline $0.71$~m from the capture point, outside the tolerance of $0.25$~m (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen-case}(b)).

\subsection{Representative Capture}\label{sec:case}


Fig.~\ref{fig:case} follows a typical test task through its phases; ECC captures the target at $168.3$~s, $3.5$~s before the deadline, using $1.83$ of the $3.0$~m/s budget. In stage flight, the chaser closes from $15.4$ to $8.8$~m within $64$~s while the latitude error decreases from $39.6^{\circ}$ to $4.1^{\circ}$, so that the line of sight moves onto the sweep cone of the port; meanwhile $\psi$ grows to $169^{\circ}$ as the port turns away. Screening corrected the decisions at $32$ and $48$~s to $\ba=[1,0]^{\T}$, each with an entry predicted at $64$~s, and the entry transfer started at $66$~s. During the transfer, $\psi$ decreases as the port sweeps toward the line of sight; the entry was re-timed once, at $92$~s, and abandoned at $112$~s, after which a short hold and a further stage brought the chaser into the approach cone, and the descent started at $128$~s. The radial braking margin stays positive outside the mouth region, and $\psi$ remains below $12^{\circ}$ throughout the descent. During the descent the attitude and angular-velocity errors converge to the capture tolerances; at capture, the position error is $0.23$~m, the attitude error $0.6^{\circ}$, and the angular-velocity error $1.1\times10^{-3}$~rad/s.

\subsection{Rotation-Synchronized Guidance and Non-Learning Baselines}\label{sec:rwg-results}


\textit{1) Components of RWG:} Two components of RWG are evaluated by removing them. Entry re-timing is switched off in the trained ECC policies, both in the execution layer and in the mode prediction, and the result is compared with ECC on the same test tasks. Rotation guidance is evaluated with the fixed intent $\ba=[1,0]^{\T}$, flown once with RWG and once as the rotation-unaware approach.

Without re-timing, ECC captures $228$ of the $288$ tasks. Re-timing thus adds $2.8$~pp $[0.3, 5.6]$, positive in all three seeds, with $11$ tasks captured only with re-timing and $3$ only without it (Fig.~\ref{fig:attribution}(b)). On the $225$ tasks captured in both settings, re-timing also shortens the completion time by $1.5$~s $[1.0, 2.0]$ and reduces the velocity increment by $0.013$~m/s $[0.004, 0.022]$. Rotation guidance has the larger effect: with the same closing intent, the chaser captures $35$ of $48$ tasks under RWG but $21$ in the rotation-unaware approach, and all $27$ failures of the latter are timeouts (Fig.~\ref{fig:reference}).

\textit{2) Non-learning baselines:} Fig.~\ref{fig:reference} places ECC among the non-learning baselines. Pure MPC captures $16$ of $48$ tasks; its failures comprise $9$ timeouts, $13$ propellant exhaustions, and $10$ unsafe states, consistent with the absence of a stage layer that schedules the encounter with the port. The fixed intent and the deadline rule, which act through RWG and the execution layer, capture $35$ and $33$ tasks ($73\%$ and $69\%$), below the $81.9\%$ of ECC, whose policy adapts the closing pace and the along-sweep motion to the state. Direct entry planning captures $44$ tasks ($92\%$); it indicates the capture rate the execution layer attains when the entry planner is invoked throughout the far field instead of through the stage intents.

\subsection{Learning Process}\label{sec:learning-curves}


The learning process is recorded on the training tasks with the setting of Table~\ref{tab:scenario} and, for ECC and SAC--MPC, on $24$ validation initial conditions disjoint from the test set (Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}). In the first windows, the two screened methods capture more often than the two unscreened ones (Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}(a)), consistent with screening turning proposals that would miss a pass into entries while the policy is still exploring, so that captures and their rewards enter the replay buffer early. In the last window, ECC reaches a capture rate of $0.76$, against $0.67$--$0.71$ for the other three methods.

Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}(b) counts how often screening replaces the proposal. For ECC, the corrections per task decrease from $0.86$, $1.20$, and $1.35$ in the first window of the three seeds to $0.68$, $0.62$, and $0.94$ in the last one, while the capture rate rises; fewer proposals require a correction as training proceeds. Without the execution feedback, the correction count varies between $0.63$ and $1.29$ without a consistent decrease. On the validation set, ECC improves from $17$, $18$, and $15$ to $19$, $19$, and $20$ captures out of $24$, whereas SAC--MPC changes from $13$, $12$, and $17$ to $13$, $12$, and $18$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}(c)). The policies evaluated in the preceding subsections are those after $5000$ decisions.
