\begin{abstract}
Capturing a noncooperative target that tumbles at a few degrees per second requires a chaser to reach a docking port that faces it only once per spin period, under corridor, field-of-view, approach-speed, and actuator constraints, before a deadline and within a propellant budget. Learning-guided model predictive control can supply the long-horizon decisions of such a task, but whether a learned decision is converted into an entry toward the port is resolved by the constrained executor within seconds, after the decision has been taken. This article proposes execution-feedback closed-loop coordination (ECC) with rotation-synchronized window guidance (RWG). RWG uses the cone swept by the port to turn a two-dimensional stage intent into motion that regulates the line of sight onto the sweep and times the encounter with the port, and it plans and re-times entries toward the predicted port motion. At every decision, ECC predicts with the models of the execution layer which entry opportunities the proposed intent and its alternatives can realize, keeps, corrects, or replaces the proposal by one rule, and trains a soft actor--critic policy on the resulting closed loop, with the proposal stored so that the critics evaluate what happens when the policy proposes it. In six-degree-of-freedom simulations with gravity gradient and $J_{2}$ perturbation, ECC captures $85.4\%$ of $576$ time-limited test tasks, $11.6$ percentage points more than learning-guided model predictive control on the same execution chain at comparable completion time and velocity increment, and it retains its capture rate under a slower spin and a stronger nutation outside the training distribution.
\end{abstract}
