\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATIONS}\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates the method at four levels. Two representative tasks show the complete constrained pre-capture process and the action of coordination in a critical window (Section~\ref{sec:cases}); the test set compares ECC with learning-guided MPC on the same execution chain and with MPC alone (Section~\ref{sec:main}); controlled checks isolate RWG and the forms of coordination (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}); and the final models are evaluated across the task distribution and under conditions outside it (Section~\ref{sec:generalization}).

\subsection{Simulation Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textit{1) Scenario:} The target flies on a $500$-km circular orbit with an inclination of $45^{\circ}$, and both spacecraft are propagated with \eqref{eq:kin}--\eqref{eq:fg} by an adaptive Runge--Kutta integrator. The target is a homogeneous box with a grapple rod leading to the port and tumbles freely at a spin rate between $2.1$ and $2.7^{\circ}$/s about an axis that deviates by $10$--$20^{\circ}$ from its body $z$-axis, so that $\Ts\in[133,171]$~s; its initial attitude is uniformly distributed on $\SO$. The chaser starts in a uniformly random direction at a range of $12$--$16$~m, with a relative speed of at most $0.05$~m/s, a boresight within $15^{\circ}$ of the port, and per-axis angular rates of at most $0.005$~rad/s. Each task draws its deadline from $\mathcal{U}(140,200)$~s, so that the latest entry arrival $\te$ lies between $95$ and $155$~s and is comparable to one spin period. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters.

\textit{2) Compared methods:} SAC--MPC executes the proposed intent directly and differs from ECC only in the coordination; it is the main baseline. TD3--MPC replaces SAC by TD3 \cite{fujimoto2018td3}, with target-policy smoothing noise $0.2$ clipped at $0.5$, a policy delay of $2$, and Gaussian exploration noise of standard deviation $0.1$. The three learning methods share the execution layer, the observation \eqref{eq:obs}, the action space, the networks, the shaped reward \eqref{eq:shaping}, and the task distribution. MPC applies the near-field MPC of Section~\ref{sec:executor} from the initial instant with its reference at the capture point rotating with the target, without the decision, stage, and entry layers; the comparison with it concerns the architecture as a whole rather than the coordination alone.

\textit{3) Training and evaluation:} Each learning method is trained from zero with three seeds for $6000$ decisions. The test set consists of $192$ initial conditions that do not occur in training, drawn from the same distribution. The final model of each training run is evaluated on all of them without exploration noise, giving $576$ test tasks per learning method; MPC is evaluated once on the $192$ initial conditions and paired with each training run. All comparisons are paired on the initial conditions, and the $95\%$ confidence intervals of paired differences are obtained by a bootstrap that resamples the initial conditions within each training run ($10\,000$ resamples), so that they reflect the variation over tasks for the trained policies. The metrics are the capture rate and the causes of failure, the completion time and the velocity increment, the return $G$ of the unshaped reward \eqref{eq:reward}, the terminal errors, and the computation time of the coordination. Capture-rate differences are given in percentage points (pp).

\subsection{Training Process}\label{sec:learning-curves}


Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}(a) shows the capture rate of the behavior policies during training. ECC captures more tasks from the beginning, since coordination acts on the proposals of the initial policy, and its three runs remain closer together than those of SAC--MPC and TD3--MPC; SAC--MPC improves more over training and spreads more across runs. The share of corrected decisions of ECC stays at about one fifth (Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}(b)); between the first and the last fifth of training it is unchanged in one run and decreases in the other two ($0.171\to0.119$ and $0.190\to0.146$), while the number of decisions per task also decreases. Launched long-range entries remain at $0.10$--$0.26$ per task.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Pre-Capture Process}\label{sec:cases}

Two test tasks show the closed loop at the level of the physical state. In both, ECC and SAC--MPC are the final models of their own training runs, so that their intents differ from the first decision on.


\begin{figure}[!tb]
\centering
\includegraphics{figures/fig_res_caseB.pdf}

The triangle and the thin line mark the correction of ECC at $16$~s; the dashed and dotted vertical lines mark the latest entry arrival $\te$ and the deadline, and the solid line the capture by ECC.}
\label{fig:caseB}

\textit{1) Task A, complete pre-capture:} Fig.~\ref{fig:case} shows a task that ECC captures without a correction. Both decisions keep the proposed intent, and in stage flight the chaser closes in while the port turns away from the line of sight, so that $\psi$ grows to about $170^{\circ}$. The entry transfer starts at $32$~s at a range of $10.8$~m, while the port still faces away: the plan arrives at the mouth point as the port sweeps toward it, and $\psi$ falls within the gate during the transfer. Three re-timings, each delaying the arrival by $4$~s, keep the entry in the same pass. The descent starts at $94$~s inside the approach cone; the four errors then decrease to the capture tolerances, and the target is captured at $143.6$~s with $\Delta v=1.84$~m/s. Throughout the task, the clearance, the corridor margin, and the applicable braking and speed margins stay nonnegative, the view angle stays within $\vartheta_{\mathrm{fov}}$, and the wrench stays within its bounds, reaching them during the entry transfer and at the start of the descent (Fig.~\ref{fig:case}(d)--(f)). SAC--MPC captures the same task at $142.8$~s with $2.03$~m/s, after an entry started at $26$~s.


\textit{2) Task B, critical window:} The task starts with a latitude offset of about $-50^{\circ}$, and its deadline of $147.8$~s leaves $\te=102.8$~s (Fig.~\ref{fig:caseB}). Both methods close in and regulate the latitude offset at similar rates. At $16$~s, the proposal $[0.67,0.10]^{\T}$ of ECC reaches no event within $\Tp$, and the only critical candidate, $[1,1]^{\T}$, has a nominal entry $24$~s later; the intent is corrected to it, and the proposals at $32$ and $48$~s are kept. The entry transfer starts at $50$~s at a range of $8.6$~m, after the nominal event because the prediction is nominal, and brings $\psi$ below the gate at about $81$~s. After one re-timing at $78$~s, which delays the arrival by $2$~s with the larger thrust bound, the descent starts at $96$~s with the port nearly aligned, and the target is captured at $133.0$~s with $\Delta v=1.20$~m/s. SAC--MPC executes its intents directly and forms no entry; it reaches the descent region from stage flight at $100$~s, when $\psi$ is still about $27^{\circ}$, and the descent follows the rotating port until the deadline, ending $0.33$~m from the capture point.

\subsection{Comparative Performance}\label{sec:main}


Table~\ref{tab:main} summarizes the test set. ECC captures $492$ of $576$ tasks ($85.4\%$), against $73.8\%$ for SAC--MPC, $63.9\%$ for TD3--MPC, and $44.3\%$ for MPC. The failures of the learning methods are almost all timeouts. Paired with SAC--MPC on the same execution chain, ECC raises the capture rate by $11.6$~pp in total and in each training run ($+7$, $+38$, and $+22$ captures), captures $90$ tasks that SAC--MPC misses against $23$ in the other direction, and raises the return by $24.0$. On the $402$ tasks captured by both, the mean completion time of ECC is $1.6$~s shorter ($[0.1,3.1]$~s; median difference $+0.3$~s) and the velocity increment differs by $+0.006$~m/s ($[-0.010,0.023]$): ECC captures more tasks in time at a comparable time and propellant cost. Against TD3--MPC the gain is $21.5$~pp. MPC alone violates a constraint or exhausts its propellant in $59$ of its $192$ tasks; on the $203$ paired tasks captured by both, ECC is $28.0$~s faster ($[23.3,32.6]$) and uses $0.44$~m/s less ($[0.38,0.50]$), which reflects the stage and entry structure as a whole.

Table~\ref{tab:rwg} groups the checks by protocol. The rotation-synchronized guidance is checked with a fixed, non-learned intent, flown once with RWG and once with a stage goal that only closes along the line of sight, without latitude regulation and along-sweep motion, under the same entry logic; the check thus covers the guidance as a whole. With the same intent, the guidance raises the captures from $64$ to $103$ of $192$, and no task is captured only without it. Entry re-timing is switched off at evaluation in the final ECC models, which then plan only to the original arrival instant; it adds $2.1$~pp, a small effect that comes mostly from one training run ($+11$ captures). The last two rows compare separately trained policies. Coordination with both kinds of opportunities raises the capture rate over direct execution; a variant that keeps and launches but never corrects captures $13$ tasks more than ECC, and it improves on SAC--MPC by $13.9$~pp $[10.4,17.2]$. The gain is therefore carried by coordinating the decisions with the opportunities the execution layer can realize, and not by the correction branch on its own.

At the $2042$ test decisions of ECC ($745$, $645$, and $652$ in the three runs), coordination keeps the proposal at $33$--$58\%$ of the decisions, corrects it at $12$--$27\%$, launches the long-range entry at about $6\%$, and passes the proposal through at the remaining $24$--$35\%$, where neither the proposal nor a critical candidate reaches an event. The run with the most corrections also keeps the fewest proposals; across runs, coordination confirms or passes through most decisions and intervenes in about one fifth to one third of them.
