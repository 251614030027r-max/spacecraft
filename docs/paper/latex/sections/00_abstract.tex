\begin{abstract}
The pre-capture approach to a noncooperative target that tumbles at a few degrees per second is limited by time as much as by geometry: the docking port passes the chaser once per spin period, and this period is comparable to the task deadline. Learning-guided model predictive control can supply the long-horizon decisions that such a task requires, but whether a decision yields an entry toward the port is resolved later by the constrained executor, and the learned layer receives this outcome only through a sparse return. This article proposes execution-feedback closed-loop coordination (ECC) with rotation-synchronized window guidance (RWG). RWG characterizes the passing window by the cone swept by the port and reduces each stage decision to a two-dimensional intent of closing pace and along-sweep timing, while a fixed law regulates the line of sight onto the sweep. ECC returns to every decision the entry opportunities that the execution layer can realize, selects the executed intent or entry by a coordination map, and trains a soft actor--critic policy on the coordinated closed loop so that its critics evaluate the proposed intents. In six-degree-of-freedom simulations, ECC completes the pre-capture approach in $85.4\%$ of $576$ time-limited test tasks, $11.6$ percentage points more than learning-guided model predictive control on the same execution chain at comparable time and propellant cost, and it keeps this rate under a slower spin and a stronger nutation outside the training distribution.
\end{abstract}
