\begin{abstract}
The pre-capture approach to a noncooperative target requires a servicing spacecraft to reach a capture pose in front of a moving docking port under safety, corridor, field-of-view, relative-speed, and actuator constraints and within limited time and propellant. Learning-guided model predictive control (MPC) assigns the task-level decisions to a reinforcement learning policy and the constraint enforcement to MPC, but the policy decides without knowing which of its decisions the constrained execution can turn into progress toward the port. This article proposes execution-feedback closed-loop coordination (ECC) for this decision--execution interface. At every decision, the execution layer predicts the task opportunities that it can realize, a coordination map used in both training and operation selects the executed decision from the proposal of the policy and these opportunities, and a soft actor--critic policy is trained on the resulting closed loop. For a target that tumbles at a few degrees per second, so that the port passes the chaser only once or twice before the deadline, rotation-synchronized window guidance (RWG) uses the cone swept by the port to express the stage decision as a two-dimensional intent of closing pace and encounter timing, and it plans and re-times entries toward the moving port. In six-degree-of-freedom simulations of the time-limited pre-capture approach, ECC completes the approach in $85.4\%$ of $576$ test tasks, $11.6$ percentage points more than learning-guided MPC on the same execution chain, with similar time and velocity increment on the tasks that both complete, and it maintains this rate under the slower spin and the stronger nutation tested outside the training distribution.
\end{abstract}
