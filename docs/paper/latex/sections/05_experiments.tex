\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATIONS}\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates ECC and RWG on time-limited capture of the tumbling target. The simulations address three questions: whether execution-opportunity coordination raises the capture rate of learning-guided MPC on the same execution chain, whether the rotation-synchronized intent and entry re-timing of RWG are effective, and how the coordination acts in the closed loop. After the setup, the training process (Section~\ref{sec:learning-curves}) and two representative tasks (Section~\ref{sec:cases}) show the closed loop at work; the full test set then answers the first question (Section~\ref{sec:main}), and Section~\ref{sec:rwg-results} answers the other two.

\subsection{Simulation Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textit{1) Scenario:} The scenario, constraint, and task parameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:scenario}. The target flies on a $500$-km circular orbit with an inclination of $45^{\circ}$, and both spacecraft are propagated with \eqref{eq:kin}--\eqref{eq:fg} by an adaptive Runge--Kutta integrator. The target is a homogeneous box with a grapple rod leading to the port and tumbles freely at a spin rate between $2.1$ and $2.7^{\circ}$/s about an axis that deviates by $10$--$20^{\circ}$ from its body $z$-axis, so that $\Ts\in[133, 171]$~s. Its initial attitude is uniformly distributed on $\SO$. The chaser starts in a uniformly random direction at a range of $12$--$16$~m, with a relative speed of at most $0.05$~m/s, a boresight within $15^{\circ}$ of the port, and per-axis angular rates of at most $0.005$~rad/s. Each task draws its deadline from $\mathcal{U}(140,200)$~s, so that the latest admissible entry time $\te$ lies between $95$ and $155$~s and is comparable to one spin period.

\textit{2) Compared methods:} Table~\ref{tab:methods} lists the compared methods. SAC--MPC executes the proposed intent directly and differs from ECC only in the coordination; it is the main baseline. TD3--MPC replaces SAC by TD3 \cite{fujimoto2018td3} with target-policy smoothing noise $0.2$ clipped at $0.5$, a policy delay of $2$, and Gaussian exploration noise with standard deviation $0.1$. All learning methods share the execution layer, the observation \eqref{eq:obs}, the action space, the networks, the shaped reward \eqref{eq:shaping}, and the task distribution, and each is trained from scratch with three random seeds for $6000$ decisions under the parameters of Table~\ref{tab:params}. MPC applies the near-field MPC of Section~\ref{sec:executor} from the initial instant, with its reference at the capture point rotating with the target and without the decision and stage layers; it represents constrained short-horizon optimization without long-horizon decisions.

\textit{3) Evaluation:} The test set consists of $192$ initial conditions that do not occur in training. Each learning method is evaluated with the final model of each seed and the mean action, giving $576$ test tasks, and MPC is evaluated once on the $192$ initial conditions and paired with each seed. All methods are evaluated on the same initial conditions, so every comparison is paired; $95\%$ confidence intervals of paired differences are obtained by a bootstrap that resamples the initial conditions within each seed ($10\,000$ resamples). The metrics are the capture rate and the causes of failure, the completion time and the velocity increment on the tasks captured by both methods of a pair, the return $G$ of the unshaped reward \eqref{eq:reward}, and the computation time of the coordination; capture-rate differences are given in percentage points (pp).

Fig.~\ref{fig:learning} records the capture rate of the three learning methods over windows of $100$ training tasks, together with the use of coordination by ECC. From the first to the last fifth of training, the capture rate of ECC rises from $0.72$, $0.80$, and $0.81$ to $0.78$, $0.93$, and $0.86$ in the three seeds, that of SAC--MPC from $0.61$, $0.41$, and $0.56$ to $0.85$, $0.69$, and $0.77$, and that of TD3--MPC from $0.66$, $0.56$, and $0.48$ to $0.74$, $0.63$, and $0.65$. ECC thus captures more often from the beginning of training and its seeds remain closer together, whereas SAC--MPC improves more during training and spreads more across seeds. Over the same period, the corrections per task of ECC decrease from $1.23$, $0.72$, and $0.70$ to $1.17$, $0.39$, and $0.53$, while launches of the long-range entry stay rare (Fig.~\ref{fig:learning}(b)): the policy increasingly proposes intents that the execution layer realizes by itself. The training rates include exploration and are not the capture rates of the deterministic policies evaluated below.

\subsection{Representative Cases}\label{sec:cases}

Two test tasks show how the closed loop unfolds. Both use the final models of one seed; ECC and SAC--MPC are trained separately, so their intents differ from the first decision on. Task~A is captured by both methods without any correction by ECC and shows a complete capture; task~B is captured by ECC after a correction and missed by SAC--MPC.

\textit{1) Task A, complete capture:} At both decisions of ECC, the proposed intent was predicted to trigger an entry by itself and was kept. In stage flight, the chaser closes in while the port turns away from the line of sight, so that $\psi$ grows (Fig.~\ref{fig:case}(b)). The entry transfer starts at $34$~s at a range of $10.4$~m, while $\psi$ is still large: the entry plan arrives at the mouth point as the port sweeps toward it, and $\psi$ falls within the gate $\psig$ during the transfer. Tracking deviations trigger three re-timings, at $50$, $62$, and $80$~s, each shifting the arrival by $4$~s, and the entry is never lost. The descent starts at $96$~s inside the approach cone, the attitude and angular-velocity errors converge to the capture tolerances (Fig.~\ref{fig:case}(c)), and the target is captured at $143.6$~s with $\Delta v=1.84$~m/s. The radial braking margin remains positive outside the mouth region. SAC--MPC captures the same task at $142.8$~s with $2.03$~m/s, after an entry started at $28$~s and four re-timings.

\textit{2) Task B, critical window:} The task starts with a latitude offset of about $-50^{\circ}$ and a deadline of $147.8$~s (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen-case}). At $0$~s, ECC keeps its intent, which is predicted to trigger an entry $40$~s later. At $16$~s, the proposal $[0.67,0.10]^{\T}$ triggers no event within $\Tp$; the only critical candidate, $[1,1]^{\T}$, predicts an entry $24$~s later, and the intent is corrected to it (Fig.~\ref{fig:screen-case}(a)). The proposals at $32$ and $48$~s are kept. The entry transfer starts at $52$~s at a range of $8.4$~m, is re-timed once at $78$~s with the larger thrust bound, and the descent starts at $98$~s; the target is captured at $133.0$~s with $\Delta v=1.20$~m/s. SAC--MPC executes its seven intents directly and never forms an entry. It reaches the descent region from stage flight at $102$~s and times out at $147.8$~s close to the capture point. The single correction replaced one intent, $[0.67,0.10]^{\T}$ by $[1,1]^{\T}$; the policy's own intents carried the approach before and after it, and the entry that followed left time for the descent before the deadline.

Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:main} compare the four methods on the $192$ test initial conditions. ECC captures $492$ of $576$ tasks ($85.4\%$), SAC--MPC $425$ ($73.8\%$), TD3--MPC $368$ ($63.9\%$), and MPC $85$ of $192$ ($44.3\%$). The failures of the learning methods are almost all timeouts, with one unsafe outcome for ECC and two for TD3--MPC, whereas MPC also exhausts its propellant or violates a constraint in $59$ tasks. Paired on the same tasks, ECC raises the capture rate over SAC--MPC by $11.6$~pp ($95\%$ CI $[8.3, 15.1]$) and the return by $24.0$ $[17.0, 31.1]$; the gain is $+7$, $+38$, and $+22$ captures in the three seeds, and $90$ tasks are captured by ECC alone against $23$ by SAC--MPC alone. On the $402$ tasks captured by both, the median completion times differ by $0.3$~s and the median velocity increments by $0.001$~m/s. Coordination therefore raises the number of tasks captured in time and leaves the efficiency of a capture unchanged. Against TD3--MPC, the gain is $21.5$~pp $[17.7, 25.3]$ with $142$ and $18$ tasks captured by one method only. Against MPC, it is $41.1$~pp $[35.8, 46.4]$, and on the $203$ tasks captured by both, ECC is faster by $27.2$~s and uses $0.35$~m/s less in the median, consistent with the cost of tracking the rotating capture point from the initial range without a stage layer.

Of the $192$ initial conditions, $191$ are captured by at least one method and seed; ECC captures $152$ in all three seeds and $19$ in none. Each coordination takes $1.50$~s in the median, $3.75$~s at the $95$th percentile, and at most $10.36$~s over $2042$ decisions, measured under parallel load on the test computer, which is below the decision period of $16$~s.

\textit{1) RWG checks:} The two parts of RWG are checked without retraining. The rotation-synchronized intent is checked with the fixed intent $\ba=[1,0]^{\T}$, flown once with RWG and once with a stage goal that only closes along the line of sight, without latitude regulation and along-sweep motion, while the entry logic is unchanged. It is not removed from the trained policies, since that would change the meaning of their actions. Entry re-timing is switched off in the final ECC models, which then use only the original arrival time. Table~\ref{tab:rwg} gives the results. With the same intent, rotation guidance raises the captures from $64$ to $103$ of $192$, by $20.3$~pp, and no task is captured only without it. Re-timing adds $2.1$~pp, with $21$ tasks captured only with it and $9$ only without it; most of the gain comes from one seed ($+11$).

\textit{2) Deadline and spin rate:} The advantage of ECC grows as the deadline tightens (Fig.~\ref{fig:strata}). For deadlines of $140$--$155$~s, ECC captures $70.0\%$ of the tasks and SAC--MPC $56.7\%$; for $185$--$200$~s, the rates are $97.0\%$ and $93.9\%$. Tighter deadlines leave fewer passes before $\te$, which is where coordination acts. Across the three thirds of the spin-rate range, the capture rates are $84.4\%$, $87.0\%$, and $84.9\%$ for ECC and $72.4\%$, $74.0\%$, and $75.0\%$ for SAC--MPC.

\textit{3) How coordination acts:} Over the test tasks of the three seeds, coordination kept the proposal at $33.0\%$, $55.0\%$, and $58.4\%$ of the decisions, corrected it at $26.7\%$, $14.9\%$, and $12.1\%$, launched the long-range entry at $5.8\%$, $5.7\%$, and $6.0\%$, and took no action at the remaining $34.5\%$, $24.3\%$, and $23.5\%$, at which neither the proposal nor a critical opportunity reached an event. Most decisions are thus either confirmed or left to the policy; the seed with the fewest kept proposals is also the one with the most corrections and the smallest gain over SAC--MPC.
