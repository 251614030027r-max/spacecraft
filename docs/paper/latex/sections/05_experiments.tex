\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATIONS}\label{sec:experiments}

This section first shows the training process (Section~\ref{sec:learning-curves}) and the complete constrained pre-capture process in two representative tasks (Section~\ref{sec:cases}). It then compares ECC on the test set with learning-guided MPC on the same execution chain and with MPC alone (Section~\ref{sec:main}), checks the rotation-synchronized guidance and the entry re-timing (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}), and evaluates the final models across task conditions, including two conditions outside the training distribution (Section~\ref{sec:generalization}).

\subsection{Simulation Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textit{1) Scenario:} The target flies on a $500$-km circular orbit with an inclination of $45^{\circ}$, and both spacecraft are propagated with \eqref{eq:kin}--\eqref{eq:fg} by an adaptive Runge--Kutta integrator. The target is a homogeneous box with a grapple rod leading to the port and tumbles freely at a spin rate between $2.1$ and $2.7^{\circ}$/s about an axis that deviates by $10$--$20^{\circ}$ from its body $z$-axis, so that $\Ts\in[133,171]$~s; its initial attitude is uniformly distributed on $\SO$. The chaser parameters follow \cite{yang2025taes}. The chaser starts in a uniformly random direction at a range of $12$--$16$~m, with a relative speed of at most $0.05$~m/s, a boresight within $15^{\circ}$ of the port, and per-axis angular rates of at most $0.005$~rad/s. Each task draws its deadline from $\mathcal{U}(140,200)$~s, so that the latest entry arrival $\te$ lies between $95$ and $155$~s and is comparable to one spin period. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the main parameters, and Table~\ref{tab:params2} in Appendix~\ref{app:params} the remaining parameters of the execution layer and of training.

\textit{2) Compared methods:} SAC--MPC executes the proposed intent directly and differs from ECC only in the coordination; it is the main baseline. TD3--MPC replaces SAC by TD3 \cite{fujimoto2018td3}, with target-policy smoothing noise $0.2$ clipped at $0.5$, a policy delay of $2$, and Gaussian exploration noise of standard deviation $0.1$. The three learning methods share the execution layer, the observation \eqref{eq:obs}, the action space, the networks, the shaped reward \eqref{eq:shaping}, and the task distribution. MPC applies the near-field MPC of Section~\ref{sec:executor} from the initial instant with its reference at the capture point rotating with the target, without the decision, stage, and entry layers, and thus represents a single-layer constrained controller.

\textit{3) Training and evaluation:} Each learning method is trained from zero with three seeds for $6000$ decisions. The test set consists of $192$ initial conditions that do not occur in training, drawn from the same distribution. The final model of each training run is evaluated on all of them without exploration noise, giving $576$ test tasks per learning method; MPC is evaluated once on the $192$ initial conditions and paired with each training run. Comparisons are paired on the initial conditions, and the $95\%$ confidence intervals of paired differences are obtained by a bootstrap that resamples the initial conditions within each training run ($10\,000$ resamples). The metrics are the capture rate and the causes of failure, the completion time and the velocity increment of captured tasks, and the return $G$ of the unshaped reward \eqref{eq:reward}; differences of capture rates are given in percentage points (pp).

\subsection{Training Process}\label{sec:learning-curves}

Fig.~\ref{fig:learning} shows the capture rate of the behavior policies during training. ECC captures more tasks from the first windows on, because coordination acts on the proposals of the initial policy, and its three runs stay closer together than those of the baselines. SAC--MPC improves more over training and spreads more across runs, and TD3--MPC remains below both. In ECC, corrections occur at $12$--$28\%$ of the decisions and decrease in two of the three runs over training, and launched long-range entries occur $0.10$--$0.26$ times per task.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Pre-Capture Process}\label{sec:cases}

Two test tasks show the closed loop at the level of the physical state, selected by rules fixed in advance: Task~A is a task that ECC captures without any correction and SAC--MPC also captures, and Task~B is a task in which ECC corrects an intent and captures whereas SAC--MPC times out. ECC and SAC--MPC are the final models of their own training runs.

\textit{1) Task A, complete pre-capture:} Both decisions of ECC keep the proposed intent (Fig.~\ref{fig:caseA}). In stage flight, the chaser closes in while the port turns away from the line of sight, and $\psi$ grows to about $170^{\circ}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:caseA-con}(a)). The entry transfer starts at $32$~s at a range of $10.8$~m while the port still faces away; the plan arrives at the mouth point as the port sweeps toward it, so that the chaser travels around the target in $\FT$ and $\psi$ falls below the gate during the transfer. Three re-timings, each delaying the arrival by $4$~s, keep the entry in the same pass. The terminal descent starts at $94$~s inside the approach cone, the four errors decrease into the capture tolerances, and the capture follows at $143.6$~s after the tolerances have held for $1$~s, with $\Delta v=1.84$~m/s. Over the task, the collision clearance stays above $0.50$~m and the corridor margin above $0.48$~m, the view angle stays below $26^{\circ}$, and the radial braking margin remains positive outside the mouth region, as do the axial braking and relative-speed margins inside it. The force and the torque saturate briefly when the reference changes, at the start of the task and of the entry transfer, at the re-timings, and at the start of the descent, and stay below them otherwise (Fig.~\ref{fig:caseA-con}(b)). SAC--MPC captures the same task at $142.8$~s with $2.03$~m/s, after an entry started at $26$~s.

\textit{2) Task B, critical window:} The task starts with a latitude offset of about $-50^{\circ}$, and its deadline of $147.8$~s leaves $\te=102.8$~s (Fig.~\ref{fig:caseB}). Both methods close in and regulate the latitude offset at similar rates. At $16$~s, the proposal $[0.67,0.10]^{\T}$ of ECC reaches no event within $\Tp$, and the only critical candidate, $[1,1]^{\T}$, predicts an entry $24$~s later; the intent is corrected to it, and the proposals at $32$ and $48$~s are kept. The entry transfer starts at $50$~s at a range of $8.6$~m, after the nominal event because the prediction is nominal, and $\psi$ falls below the gate at about $81$~s. After one re-timing at $78$~s, which delays the arrival by $2$~s with the larger thrust bound, the descent starts at $96$~s with the port nearly aligned, and the target is captured at $133.0$~s with $\Delta v=1.20$~m/s, at a distance of $0.19$~m from the capture point. SAC--MPC executes its intents directly and forms no entry: $\psi$ falls below the gate only at about $95$~s, the chaser reaches the descent region from stage flight at $100$~s with $\psi$ still near $27^{\circ}$, and the descent follows the rotating port until the deadline, ending $0.33$~m from the capture point.

\subsection{Comparative Performance}\label{sec:main}

Fig.~\ref{fig:outcome}(a) shows the outcomes on the test set. ECC captures $492$ of $576$ tasks ($85.4\%$), against $73.8\%$ for SAC--MPC, $63.9\%$ for TD3--MPC, and $44.3\%$ for MPC, and the failures of the learning methods are almost all timeouts. Paired with SAC--MPC on the same execution chain, ECC raises the capture rate by $11.6$~pp $[8.3,15.1]$, capturing $90$ tasks that SAC--MPC misses against $23$ in the other direction, and it is ahead in each of the three training runs; the mean return rises by $24.0$ $[17.0,31.1]$. Against TD3--MPC, the gain is $21.5$~pp $[17.7,25.3]$.

The completion times and velocity increments of the three learning methods lie close together (Fig.~\ref{fig:outcome}(b) and (c)). On the $402$ tasks that ECC and SAC--MPC both capture, the mean completion time of ECC differs by $-1.6$~s $[-3.1,-0.1]$ and the mean velocity increment by $+0.006$~m/s $[-0.010,0.023]$. The gain of ECC therefore consists of more tasks captured in time at a comparable cost per capture. MPC alone fails through constraint violations and propellant exhaustion as well as timeouts; on the $203$ tasks captured by both, ECC is $28.0$~s faster $[23.3,32.6]$ and uses $0.44$~m/s $[0.38,0.50]$ less, which reflects the stage and entry structure as a whole. Each coordination takes a wall-clock time of $1.50$~s in the median, $3.75$~s at the $95$th percentile, and at most $10.36$~s over the $2042$ test decisions, measured on a desktop computer under parallel evaluation load, all below the decision period of $16$~s.

Table~\ref{tab:rwg} lists two checks of the execution layer. The rotation-synchronized guidance is checked with a fixed, non-learned intent, flown once with RWG and once with a stage goal that only closes along the line of sight, without latitude regulation and along-sweep motion, under the same entry logic. With the same intent, the guidance raises the captures from $64$ to $103$ of $192$, and no task is captured only without it; the latitude regulation that brings the line of sight onto the sweep cone (Lemma~\ref{lem:window}) is the core of this effect. Entry re-timing is switched off at evaluation in the final ECC models, which then plan only to the original arrival instant; it has a small, positive effect of $2.1$~pp. Coordination intervenes, by correcting an intent or launching the long-range entry, at $18$--$33\%$ of the test decisions in the three training runs; the gain of Section~\ref{sec:main} is that of the closed loop formed by coordination and learning on top of this execution layer. Appendix~\ref{app:nocorr} reports a separately trained variant of the coordination without the critical-window correction.

\subsection{Performance Across Task Conditions}\label{sec:generalization}

\textit{1) Within the training distribution:} The gain of ECC over SAC--MPC is $13.3$, $15.9$, and $13.4$~pp in the three tighter deadline bins and $3.1$~pp for deadlines of $185$--$200$~s, where both methods capture more than $93\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cond}(a)). Tighter deadlines leave fewer passes before $\te$, which is where coordination acts. Across the thirds of the spin-rate range, the capture rates of both methods change by less than $3$~pp (Fig.~\ref{fig:cond}(b)).

\textit{2) Outside the training distribution:} The final models of ECC and SAC--MPC are evaluated without retraining on the same $192$ initial conditions, with one parameter distribution changed at a time. The two conditions stress the two assumptions on which the opportunity structure rests. A slower spin of $1.5$--$2.1^{\circ}$/s lengthens $\Ts$, so that the same deadlines leave a smaller opportunity margin $\eta$ in \eqref{eq:eta} and a missed pass costs more time. A spin-axis offset of $20$--$40^{\circ}$ strengthens the nutation and thus the departure of the truth motion from the constant-rate prediction \eqref{eq:pred} used by coordination and planning. With the slower spin, ECC captures $85.6\%$ of the tasks and SAC--MPC $63.2\%$, a paired difference of $22.4$~pp $[18.6,26.2]$; ECC keeps its nominal capture rate, whereas SAC--MPC loses $10.6$~pp. With the stronger nutation, ECC captures $86.5\%$ and SAC--MPC $72.0\%$, a difference of $14.4$~pp $[10.8,18.1]$. In both conditions, nearly all failures are timeouts, and on the tasks captured by both methods the mean differences in completion time and velocity increment have confidence intervals that contain zero.
